Our story

From 1996 to today

1968

Rotaract is born

Rotary International launches the Rotaract programme for young people on 13 March 1968. Today there are more than 10,000 clubs in 180 countries.

1996

Anıttepe Rotaract is founded

In November 1996 we start out as one of Ankara's Rotaract clubs, sponsored by the Rotary Club of Ankara Anıttepe.

2007

The first Hello New Term Party

Our party that opens every new term and brings clubs together begins. In 2026 it was held for the 20th time.

Every year

Traditions grow

Red and White Everywhere, Balloon Fairies and Bordo become projects passed down from one generation of members to the next.

2026

Create Lasting Impact

On 1 July the board led by President Simay Oğuzkurt takes office. In October, for the Republic's 103rd year, we take to the streets with 103 flags.

How we decide

Every club in the Rotary family asks the same four questions and serves in the same seven areas. We choose our projects by them too.

Rotary's 7 areas of focus

Every Rotary and Rotaract club in the world runs its service projects in these seven areas.

  • Promoting peaceBuilding dialogue and mutual understanding.
  • Fighting diseasePreventing disease, polio above all, and widening access to treatment.
  • Clean water, sanitation and hygieneAccess to safe water and healthy living conditions.
  • Saving mothers and childrenHealthy lives for mothers and young children.
  • Supporting educationEvery child able to reach school and learning.
  • Growing local economiesSupporting local economies and job skills.
  • Protecting the environmentProtecting natural resources and spreading sustainable living.

    FAQ

    Questions you might have

    What's the difference between Rotaract and Rotary?

    Rotaract is Rotary International's club programme for young people aged 18 and over. Rotary clubs are usually made up of experienced business and professional people; Rotaract clubs are made up of university students and young professionals. We share the same goals: service, leadership and international friendship.

    Do I need to be a student to join?

    No. Students and working people can both join. What matters is taking part in meetings and projects regularly.

    How do I come to a meeting?

    We meet every other Thursday at 7:30 pm at Hybrid Hub. Just fill in the guest application form and pick a meeting; we'll meet you at the door. Meetings are held in Turkish, and members are happy to help in English.

    What can I do in the club?

    You can join one of our committees: service projects, international service, vocational service, disaster response, public relations, membership or hobbies. Each committee is led by a member.
